Glossary

The words used across StockWatch, and what each one means.

Organising

Watchlist

A named list of stocks, ETFs or indexes. A stock can be in several watchlists; alerts are shared, so you never receive the same alert twice.

Organising

Follow

Switching on alerts for something: a pattern on a stock, its outlook, or its insider and congressional trades. Unfollow switches them off.

What StockWatch finds

Signal

A chart pattern (candlestick, Elliott Wave or harmonic) detected on a completed candle for a stock you follow. Every signal stays in Technical signals.

What StockWatch finds

Ticker alert

A newly filed purchase or sale by a company insider or a member of Congress, for a stock you follow.

What StockWatch finds

Outlook

The automated technical outlook: one overall buy, neutral or sell reading built from many indicators, per interval.

How you are told

Alert

The notification you receive about a signal, outlook change or ticker alert: in Latest signals and, if switched on, by email.

Reading a signal

Setup score

How much technical evidence supports a pattern, out of 100. It is not the chance of a profitable trade.

Reading a signal

Trade outcome

What price did after the signal, measured with the pattern's stop, target and time limit. It is tracked separately from the score.

Reading a signal

Status

Where a signal is in its life: potential or detected, then confirmed, invalidated or expired once later candles decide it.

Practice

Demo trade

A practice buy or sell recorded at the current quote. No real order is placed and no money moves.

Guide / 01

How a signal is detected

A pattern must meet its detection rules before it receives a score. Analysis uses completed candles.

  1. 01 Find the stock

    Search by company name or ticker and choose the correct listing.

  2. 02 Load price history

    Load available price history from market-data providers.

  3. 03 Use completed candles

    Exclude the still-forming daily, weekly, or monthly candle before running any detector.

  4. 04 Calculate indicators

    Calculate interval-specific RSI, CCI, EMA, SMA, MACD, ATR, Bollinger, VWAP, and volume context.

  5. 05 Apply structural rules

    Require exact candle geometry and prior trend, or alternating Elliott pivots and wave constraints.

  6. 06 Explain and monitor

    Return the evidence breakdown, evaluate follow-through, and notify only the combinations the user chose.

Completed-candle policy

When candles become available

Daily candles become eligible on the next configured local day. Weekly candles remain protected during the trading week and become eligible from Saturday. Monthly candles become eligible on the first day of the next month.

Supported analysis intervals

Periods follow the chart interval

Candlestick and Elliott analysis support daily, weekly and monthly bars. Indicator periods are expressed in those native bars, not silently converted from days.

Explainability

What each signal records

Each signal records its formation, direction, score version, component evidence, signal candle, close, and lifecycle state so the result can be inspected later.

Candlestick detection: exact requirements

A pattern must meet its shape and prior-trend rules before it receives a score. Detailed defaults are listed below; your settings may differ.

View candlestick rules and formulas
Real body | close − open |

The directional portion between open and close.

Full range high − low

The complete candle, including both shadows.

Upper shadow high − max(open, close)

Price explored above the real body.

Lower shadow min(open, close) − low

Price explored below the real body.

The prior-trend gate

Up to five completed candles immediately before the formation are examined; at least three are required. A trend needs a net move of at least 1.5%, at least 60% directional close or high/low transitions, and—when close-only structure is used— directional efficiency of at least 35%. Three White Soldiers may also begin from a compact base no wider than 4%.

Separate Elliott Wave analysis

Elliott Wave structures remain available as an independent weekly and monthly detector. They do not add to or subtract from the candlestick V4 setup score. Keeping the two analyses separate makes each signal explainable and avoids treating an experimental wave interpretation as candlestick confirmation.

Guide / 05

Elliott Wave detection rules

The detector finds turning points on daily, weekly and monthly charts, checks them against Elliott Wave rules, and waits for confirmation.

View Elliott Wave rules

Detection model: ELLIOTT_V1.

1 / Price history

At least 34 completed candles

Incomplete or invalid OHLCV bars are removed and the remaining candles are sorted before structural analysis.

2 / Turning points

Four volatility sensitivities

Sensitivities 0.75, 1.25, 2.0, and 3.0 seek alternating highs and lows. A reversal must exceed the greater of ATR × sensitivity or 1.25% of close × sensitivity.

3 / Impulse ordering

Higher or lower pivot structure

A bullish impulse requires higher wave I and III highs, wave II above origin, wave IV above waves II and I, and positive wave II/IV retracements below 100%. Bearish rules mirror this.

4 / Timing

Distinct legs

Each pivot leg must span at least two candles. A 15-candle impulse is preferred; compressed structures receive a quality penalty.

5 / Wave III rule

Not the shortest motive wave

For a completed impulse, Wave III must be at least the shorter of Waves I and V. A Wave III below 80% of Wave I is penalized during preliminary scoring.

6 / Present relevance

Recent confirmations

Impulse breakout, Wave V reversal, or Wave C confirmation must occur on the latest candle or remain active with follow-through inside the configured one-candle present window.

Impulse and Wave V outputs

  • Developing impulse: price closes 0.3% beyond Wave III from the Wave IV pivot, or below it for bearish structure.
  • Wave V end: a complete impulse is followed by a current reversal beyond the previous candle’s low/high.
  • Truncated Wave V: Wave V fails to exceed Wave III and receives an 18-point confidence penalty.
  • Action direction: impulse continuation follows the impulse; a Wave V end points in the reversal direction.

A-B-C correction outputs

  • A complete five-wave impulse must precede three distinct A, B, and C legs.
  • Wave A moves against the impulse but must remain beyond the original Wave 0.
  • Wave B recovers toward the impulse direction; recovery is limited to 2× Wave A.
  • Wave C moves against B; the complete correction must retrace more than 0% and less than 100% of the impulse.
  • A bullish correction requires a close back above the previous candle high; bearish requires a close below the previous low.

Variants and quality

Standard, expanded-flat, and running-flat corrections are classified from Waves B and C. Expanded flats lose 4 points; running flats lose 14. Historical overlays require structure quality ≥ 68 and suppress candidates that overlap a better structure by more than 40%. Alert emission requires Elliott confidence ≥ 75.

DeliveryEmail the matched direction

Notifications contain the pattern, interval, close, score version, and recorded evidence.

1Detected

The candlestick trade opens at the detection close; single-candle candidates first need their mandatory next-candle gate.

2Confirmed

A completed candle closes at or beyond the interval R:R profit target, successfully closing the candlestick trade.

3Invalidated

A completed candle closes at or beyond the configured stop loss, closing the candlestick trade at a loss.

4Expired

Neither level was reached within the saved horizon (8 candles daily and weekly, 6 monthly), so that candle’s close ends the candlestick trade. Elliott retains its own configured window.

Close-based, not intraday

Intraday high/low touches do not resolve lifecycle state. Only a completed candle’s close is evaluated; a close exactly on the target or stop counts as reaching that level.

Elliott Wave boundaries

A Wave V setup confirms beyond Wave IV, while a completed correction confirms beyond Wave B. A later Wave V or C extension revises the same cycle and restarts its observation window without another detection email. Invalidation is reserved for a hard Elliott rule failure; a completed ABC correction remains a separate signal stage within the same cycle.

Historical outcomes

Historical scans are recalculated on demand and are not persisted as alert events. The selected candle count controls the visible research window.

1–750 candles

User-selected lookback

The interval picker is followed by a candle-count picker and an estimated earliest date. If the ticker has less history, all available completed candles are analyzed.

Daily

Default: 1:2

The daily target uses the account's configured reward-to-risk ratio. Both the ratio and each pattern's stop-loss rule can be changed in settings.

Weekly

Default: 1:3

The weekly target uses its own account-level ratio and the selected pattern's configured stop loss.

Monthly

Default: 1:3

The monthly target is configured independently from the daily and weekly ratios.

ATR circuit breaker

25% daily · 50% weekly/monthly

If the configured stop implies a larger target move, the default plan switches to frozen ATR(14) × 1.5 risk, capped again at the interval percentage. Every value and the enable switch can be changed in candlestick settings.

Confirmed

A completed close reaches the R:R profit target.

Invalidated

A completed close reaches the configured stop loss.

Expired

Neither level wins, so the close at the end of the saved horizon ends the trade.

Pending

The trade is still open and fewer than eight outcome candles are complete.

Historical lifecycle uses completed closes as deterministic entry and exit observations. It does not model spread, slippage, fees, taxes, borrow availability, dividends, position sizing, capital limits, or overlapping trades.

Guide / 08

Limits of the analysis

Keep these limitations in mind when reviewing signals.

01

Scores are not probabilities

The candlestick score orders recorded confluence. Existing validation does not support reading 88/100 as an 88% chance of success.

02

Pattern names are classifications

Correct geometric classification does not prove that the following price move will match the assigned BUY or SELL/SHORT direction.

03

Elliott interpretation remains heuristic

Volatility-scaled pivots improve repeatability, but different sensitivity choices can still produce competing valid structures.

04

Market data can be incomplete

Listings have finite histories, providers can differ, and recently formed candles may be revised before their period completes.

05

Historical outcomes are simplified

Fixed close-to-close measurements are not executable portfolio simulations and do not include trading frictions.

06

Company disclosures arrive later

Congressional filings can be reported up to 45 days after a transaction. The temporary API Ninjas free response omits the insider transaction date, so StockWatch labels the filing date as a fallback; returns are measured from the first completed close after the filing became public, the earliest price a follower could act on, not from the insider's own execution.
