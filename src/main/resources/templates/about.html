Guide / 01

From a ticker to an explainable signal

Detection is a sequence of gates. Later evidence cannot rescue an invalid formation, and an active candle is never treated as if it has already closed.

  1. 01 Resolve the instrument

    Search stocks, ETFs, and indexes, normalize the symbol, and retain the relevant market identity.

  2. 02 Refresh and cache candles

    Request market data, fall back to the secondary provider when necessary, and persist changed bars.

  3. 03 Keep completed periods

    Exclude the still-forming daily, weekly, or monthly candle before running any detector.

  4. 04 Enrich the price series

    Calculate interval-specific RSI, CCI, EMA, SMA, MACD, ATR, Bollinger, VWAP, and volume context.

  5. 05 Apply structural rules

    Require exact candle geometry and prior trend, or alternating Elliott pivots and wave constraints.

  6. 06 Explain and monitor

    Return the evidence breakdown, evaluate follow-through, and notify only the combinations the user chose.

Completed-candle policy

No intraperiod guessing

Daily candles become eligible on the next configured local day. Weekly candles remain protected during the trading week and become eligible from Saturday. Monthly candles become eligible on the first day of the next month.

Supported analysis intervals

Native-bar calculations

Candlestick analysis supports daily, weekly, and monthly bars. Elliott analysis uses weekly and monthly bars. Indicator periods are expressed in those native bars, not silently converted from days.

Explainability

Rules before labels

Each signal records its formation, direction, score version, component evidence, signal candle, close, and lifecycle state so the result can be inspected later.

Candlestick detection: exact requirements

Version CANDLE_V4_EXPERIMENTAL first validates formation geometry and the required prior trend. The score is calculated only after the pattern exists.

Real body | close − open |

The directional portion between open and close.

Full range high − low

The complete candle, including both shadows.

Upper shadow high − max(open, close)

Price explored above the real body.

Lower shadow min(open, close) − low

Price explored below the real body.

The prior-trend gate

Up to five completed candles immediately before the formation are examined; at least three are required. A trend needs a net move of at least 1.5%, at least 60% directional close or high/low transitions, and—when close-only structure is used— directional efficiency of at least 35%. Three White Soldiers may also begin from a compact base no wider than 4%.

Separate Elliott Wave analysis

Elliott Wave structures remain available as an independent weekly and monthly detector. They do not add to or subtract from the candlestick V4 setup score. Keeping the two analyses separate makes each signal explainable and avoids treating an experimental wave interpretation as candlestick confirmation.

Guide / 05

Elliott Wave detection: structure before story

Version ELLIOTT_V1 searches weekly and monthly enriched candles. It does not draw waves by eye: it extracts alternating volatility-scaled pivots, tests ordered structures, and requires a present confirmation.

1 / Input gate

At least 34 completed candles

Incomplete or invalid OHLCV bars are removed and the remaining candles are sorted before structural analysis.

2 / Pivot engine

Four volatility sensitivities

Sensitivities 0.75, 1.25, 2.0, and 3.0 seek alternating highs and lows. A reversal must exceed the greater of ATR × sensitivity or 1.25% of close × sensitivity.

3 / Impulse ordering

Higher or lower pivot structure

A bullish impulse requires higher wave I and III highs, wave II above origin, wave IV above waves II and I, and positive wave II/IV retracements below 100%. Bearish rules mirror this.

4 / Timing

Distinct legs

Each pivot leg must span at least two candles. A 15-candle impulse is preferred; compressed structures receive a quality penalty.

5 / Wave III rule

Not the shortest motive wave

For a completed impulse, Wave III must be at least the shorter of Waves I and V. A Wave III below 80% of Wave I is penalized during preliminary scoring.

6 / Present relevance

No stale wave alerts

Impulse breakout, Wave V reversal, or Wave C confirmation must occur on the latest candle or remain active with follow-through inside the configured one-candle present window.

Impulse and Wave V outputs

  • Developing impulse: price closes 0.3% beyond Wave III from the Wave IV pivot, or below it for bearish structure.
  • Wave V end: a complete impulse is followed by a current reversal beyond the previous candle’s low/high.
  • Truncated Wave V: Wave V fails to exceed Wave III and receives an 18-point confidence penalty.
  • Action direction: impulse continuation follows the impulse; a Wave V end points in the reversal direction.

A-B-C correction outputs

  • A complete five-wave impulse must precede three distinct A, B, and C legs.
  • Wave A moves against the impulse but must remain beyond the original Wave 0.
  • Wave B recovers toward the impulse direction; recovery is limited to 2× Wave A.
  • Wave C moves against B; the complete correction must retrace more than 0% and less than 100% of the impulse.
  • A bullish correction requires a close back above the previous candle high; bearish requires a close below the previous low.

Variants and quality

Standard, expanded-flat, and running-flat corrections are classified from Waves B and C. Expanded flats lose 4 points; running flats lose 14. Historical overlays require structure quality ≥ 68 and suppress candidates that overlap a better structure by more than 40%. Alert emission requires Elliott confidence ≥ 75.

Guide / 07

Historical research without pretending it is a trade

Historical scans are recalculated on demand and are not persisted as alert events. The selected candle count controls the visible research window.

1–750 candles

User-selected lookback

The interval picker is followed by a candle-count picker and an estimated earliest date. If the ticker has less history, all available completed candles are analyzed.

Daily

10-session outcome

Success requires at least 3% in the detected direction at the fixed evaluation close.

Weekly

4-week outcome

Success requires at least 4% in the detected direction at the fourth completed weekly close.

Monthly

3-month outcome

Success requires at least 6% in the detected direction at the third completed monthly close.

Successful

Directional return reaches or exceeds the interval threshold.

Unsuccessful

Directional return reaches the same threshold in the opposite direction.

Inconclusive

The fixed-horizon close stays between the positive and negative thresholds.

Pending

Not enough later completed candles exist to evaluate the fixed horizon.

Historical impact is a close-to-close research measurement. It does not model entry delay, spread, slippage, fees, taxes, borrow availability, dividends, stop execution, position sizing, capital limits, or overlapping trades.

Guide / 08

Read the signal, keep the uncertainty

Transparency is part of the product. These boundaries matter as much as the detector rules themselves.

01

Scores are not probabilities

The candlestick score orders recorded confluence. Existing validation does not support reading 88/100 as an 88% chance of success.

02

Pattern names are classifications

Correct geometric classification does not prove that the following price move will match the assigned BUY or SELL direction.

03

Elliott interpretation remains heuristic

Volatility-scaled pivots improve repeatability, but different sensitivity choices can still produce competing valid structures.

04

Market data can be incomplete

Listings have finite histories, providers can differ, and recently formed candles may be revised before their period completes.

05

Historical outcomes are simplified

Fixed close-to-close measurements are not executable portfolio simulations and do not include trading frictions.

06

Company disclosures arrive later

Congressional filings can be reported up to 45 days after a transaction. The temporary API Ninjas free response omits the insider transaction date, so StockWatch labels the filing date as a fallback; returns still use filed prices and completed closes, not an investor's actual execution.
